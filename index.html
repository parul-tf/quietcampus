Acoustic Mathematics & Sound Physics

Why normal math fails for decibels, and how we measure physical acoustic energy.

Why Normal Math Fails

Sound pressure is logarithmic. You cannot add readings together and divide. 60 dBA + 60 dBA ≠ 120 dBA—it equals 63 dBA! We convert decibels into acoustic energy, average that energy, and convert back.

The 3-Decibel Difference Rule

A 3-decibel drop might seem small on paper, but in acoustic physics: a drop of 3 dBA cuts physical sound energy in half! Our 6.8 to 7.2 dBA drops cut sound energy by over 78%.

Spreadsheet Logarithmic Formula

=10*LOG10((10^(C2/10) + 10^(D2/10) + 10^(E2/10)) / 3)

Applied across Morning, Lunch, and Afternoon 10-minute sessions.

TREP Project Concepts Guiding Our Decisions

How The Scout Mindset, Calling Bullshit, and Factfulness shaped our project methodology.

Julia Galef

The Scout Mindset

We maintained complete intellectual honesty with our noise readings. If we initially hypothesized the Southern food stalls were the loudest, but data proved the library entrance had worse transit spikes, we report the library truth without cherry-picking.

Bergstrom & West

Calling Bullshit

We refuse to manipulate our charts. We do not truncate bar chart axes to make tiny decibel differences look artificially dramatic. We are transparent that we use student smartphones rather than pretending they are calibrated lab meters.

Hans Rosling

Factfulness

Never jump to conclusions based on a single dramatic event. When a loud car horn spikes decibels for 2 seconds, we do not label the zone as 'dangerously loud forever.' We integrate true continuous averages (LAeq) over the full 10 minutes.

Three Physical & Behavioral Nudges

Gentle, non-punitive interventions to build a sustainable, quiet campus community.

Traffic Lights for Sound

Visual Noise Decibel Displays

Color-coded monitors outside the LRC and P Block. Quiet conversations show Green (<55 dB). Exceeding 65 dB turns Yellow/Red, providing a subtle visual reminder without security shushing people.

GPS Navigational Bypass

'Quiet Route' Campus Maps

Digital maps highlighting the quietest walking paths and outdoor silent study nooks, helping students avoid the loud lunch crowds around the Southern Stall.

Vendor Deliveries & Generators

Scheduled 'Quiet Hours'

Restricting noisy delivery trucks and generator tests at the Southern Stall (B02/B03) to non-peak hours (early morning or late evening) rather than peak class transitions.
